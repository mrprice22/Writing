I never chose the breath before this one.
It came from cells that came from suns,
from every meal, from every fright,
from a mother's voice on some far night.

Call it will, call it choice, call it fate —
the moment moves before you calculate.
By the time you feel the thought arrive,
the body already knows how to drive.

So I stopped pretending I stand apart,
some ghost behind the ribs, a separate spark
that watches gears and somehow stays unbound —
no. I am the gears. I am the sound
the whole machine makes as it turns and learns,
the wave that rises, breaks, returns.

And here is the strange gift in giving up
the myth of an empty, floating cup
that fills itself from nowhere, out of nothing:
now every choice has roots. Now every failing
has a cause I can trace, a thread I can hold,
a lesson folded into the story told.

I did not do better because I could not yet.
Tomorrow I might, if today I set
the causes running — practice, rest, attention —
not by breaking chains, but by extension,
by feeding the river further upstream
so it carves a different bed downstream.

I lost a freedom that was never real.
I gained a clearer way to feel
how deep the roots go, how far back the start —
and found, past there, an older kind of art:
not standing outside the flow of things,
but being the flow, and the song it sings.
